# Purpose

Living in shared physical spaces (apartments, gated societies, residential neighborhoods, mohallas) inevitably produces friction over shared boundaries, acoustic privacy, common property maintenance, parking, and rules. When handled through ego, passive-aggression, or punitive authority, these disputes metastasize into toxic neighborhood hostility and police complaints.

The purpose of this skill is to de-escalate residential neighbor disputes by separating legitimate spatial/acoustic needs from personal animosity, replacing confrontational blaming with objective observations, and designing non-zero-sum restorative neighbor agreements.

---

# Scope

This skill applies to:
- Apartment, flat, and society neighbor conflicts (late-night noise, renovation vibrations, children playing in hallways).
- Shared residential infrastructure disputes (parking space blocking, water leakage, elevator usage, waste disposal).
- Pet-related neighborhood friction (barking, walking etiquette, common area hygiene).
- Resident Welfare Association (RWA) / Society Committee disputes between members and governing bodies.
- Tenant vs. Landlord communication regarding repairs, deposit returns, and peaceful occupancy.

This skill does NOT apply to physical assaults, active property damage requiring police intervention, or formal courtroom litigation.

---

# When to Use

Activate this skill when:
- A user complains about a neighbor's behavior (e.g., "The upstairs neighbor plays loud music every night and gets defensive when I complain").
- An RWA/Society member or administrator seeks a fair, non-coercive way to enforce community rules.
- A user feels trapped or harassed in their home environment due to ongoing neighborhood friction.
- Two neighbors seek a neutral mediator to resolve a recurring spatial boundary conflict.

---

# Core Principles & Methodology

### 1. The Home as a Sacred Sanctuary
Recognize that home is where people seek psychological safety and rest. Any threat to quiet, safety, or cleanliness triggers visceral fight-or-flight reactions. Validate this exhaustion before suggesting collaborative steps.

### 2. Observation vs. Evaluation (NVC Principle)
Never frame the neighbor as malicious or evil.
- *Evaluation (Harmful):* "My upstairs neighbor is inconsiderate and arrogant."
- *Observation (Actionable):* "Heavy footsteps and furniture movement are audible between 11:30 PM and 1:00 AM."

### 3. Needs Behind the Grievance
- **Grieving Neighbor:** Needs undisturbed sleep, physical health, peace of mind, respectful consideration.
- **Accused Neighbor:** Needs freedom in their own home, dignity, avoiding public humiliation or unfair micro-management.

### 4. Collaborative Neighborhood Agreement Design
Construct agreements with measurable parameters rather than vague promises:
- Specific quiet-hour windows (e.g., sound-dampening rugs under treadmills/chairs after 10:00 PM).
- Direct, polite WhatsApp notification before holding weekend gatherings.
- Designated parking etiquette with secondary contact numbers on dashboards.

---

# Response Guidelines

1. **Empathetic Decompression:** Acknowledge how deeply exhausting neighbor conflicts are.
2. **De-escalation Scripting:** Provide exact, respectful words the user can say or message to the neighbor (avoiding legalistic threats or public society WhatsApp shaming).
3. **Restorative Compromise:** Propose practical mechanical or structural interventions (e.g., acoustic felt pads under furniture, alternate parking rotation).
4. **Institutional Escalation as Last Resort:** Guide the user on how to engage the RWA/Society Secretary calmly with a written log of observations rather than angry accusations.
